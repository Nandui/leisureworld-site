# Page purpose and contextual-link review

Reviewed 16 September 2026. Coverage: **48 public HTML pages**, using the public-file rules in `scripts/public-files.mjs`. Each page’s actual `<main>` text, headings, anchors, action labels and relevant disclosure structure were inspected. Shared navigation is assessed once below; repeated footer links are not presented as distinct page tasks. This report complements the automated link checks and browser review; it is not a claim that external booking transactions were completed.

“Primary action” means the action that best serves the page’s purpose, including reading information where no transaction is appropriate. A page does not need an extra button merely to have a primary action. Supporting links are retained where they answer a plausible next question or complete a task.

## Shared navigation and link decisions

- **Keep** centres, activities, prices and the app in the shared navigation: they serve the most common tasks. The menu holds lower-frequency contact, training, organisational and policy routes.
- **Keep** genuine external booking, membership purchase, app-store, maps, voucher and recruitment links where their labels explain the transition. These complete tasks the informational website cannot itself perform.
- **Keep** telephone and email alternatives: they support visitors who cannot or do not want to use an app, browser account or email application.
- **Replace** old-site informational exits with local content. A source citation retained privately in audit evidence is different from sending a visitor back to old information.
- **Avoid** several buttons with different promises that all open the same generic search. The adult lesson and swim-school routes now explain the service, and the three programme pages’ assessment labels were corrected by root to lead through the assessment guidance.
- **Keep** meaningful in-page links on long timetable, price, programme and policy pages. They reduce scrolling; a short page does not need an artificial table of contents.
- **Remove or qualify** active-sounding offers when their dates are past. Camps do this explicitly; NPLQ and Douglas schedules include expiry handling.
- **Treat** a PDF as a supporting format, not a reason to preserve outdated instructions. The corrected local HTML is the visitor reference when owner-confirmed policy supersedes an old source.

## Findings raised during this pass

Root integration corrected these specific findings, and a final HTML read verified the corrections: generic assessment labels on the three programme pages; obsolete Douglas timetable wording on the swimming hub; centre-specific homepage timetable anchors; indirect teen guidance link in Help; stale enquiry-form wording/review date in the accessibility statement; and the contradictory original pool-rules poster download. These are recorded as corrections from this review, not new unanswered questions for the owner.

The remaining source limitations are already documented in `legacy-content.md`: the impossible early Tuesday Bishopstown range, conflicting peak-price boundaries and inconsistent gender-report interpretation. They are handled transparently without inventing facts. Timetables, prices, vacancies, courses and named policy contacts remain maintainable content that staff must keep current.
